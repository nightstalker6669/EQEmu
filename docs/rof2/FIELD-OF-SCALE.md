This is a qualified local implementation of original acquisition and progression routes. It does not certify every spell, class, platform, original Live statistic or natural travel/drop route.

## Acceptance qualifications

Final consumer QA retained developer account access (status 150) with the character GM flag off; the peer retained ordinary access. Those final positive consumer checks are not ordinary-status-0 access certification. Earlier normal requester denial, task and portal checks retain their own recorded scope. This batch does not add testing accounts' live privileges or live player state to public seed defaults; previously published seed content is preserved.

QA used maximum-level geared characters, a test weapon for routine actual kills, and approved position assistance in public and owned instances. Ordinary spells established the real 60/30/20-percent mechanics; no forced HP, race, spell cast, RNG, task counter or completion-history edits established acceptance. Actual original owned entrances and nearby physical exits were used, but assisted movement does not prove a complete normal travel route or collision/path coverage.

Food used four natural carcasses plus sixteen explicitly seeded QA ingredients. Some Sarnak starting actors or note sources were approved NPC-type fixtures with real loot-table RNG and actual combat. The Burynai alias note had natural-corpse provenance; the final Kurn alias note came from a post-cache-refresh NPC-type fixture. These are not static respawn-frequency or wholly natural drop proofs. Failed attempts remain distinct from later accepted runs; their kills were not transferred to new attempts.

Original encounters and acquisition routes coexist with explicitly approved local numeric reconstructions: reward floors/caps, population-decay policy, XP/faction bases, NPC balance, bounds and some placement geometry. These choices are not recovered exact Sony Live balance or historical coordinates. Dialogue is paraphrased. No client assets, private player records, credentials, raw logs, fixture tools or server binaries are part of the source publication.
